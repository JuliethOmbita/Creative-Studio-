# Respira Conmigo: research notes and game design

A guide for the breathwork coach. It covers how breathing helps kids, which tools researchers and educators already use, how to adapt them by age, and more game ideas for future versions of the program.

> These notes come from published programs and studies. They are a design starting point, not medical advice. Check the citations before using them in marketing materials, and ask a pediatric or mental-health professional to review the program.

---

## 1. Core principles (the "why")

| Principle | What it means for the game |
|---|---|
| **Long exhale = brake pedal.** When the exhale is longer than the inhale, the heart slows a little (respiratory sinus arrhythmia) and the parasympathetic system becomes more active. | Almost every exercise makes the exhale longer than the inhale (`out` weight of 1.2–1.6×). |
| **Slow breathing (~6/min) and heart-rate variability.** Slow, paced breathing increases HRV. It is the basis of HRV biofeedback (Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014; review in Zaccaro et al., 2018). | The teen "ola" (wave) exercise breathes at 5 s in and 5 s out. |
| **Name it to tame it.** Putting feelings into words reduces amygdala activity (Lieberman et al., 2007). Dan Siegel popularized this as "name it to tame it". Kids with a richer emotion vocabulary ("emotional granularity", Lisa Feldman Barrett) regulate better. | The check-in comes before the breathing. From age 9, kids choose specific words (frustración, celos, inquietud…). |
| **Interoception.** Noticing body signals (heartbeat, tight hands, butterflies) is a skill you can learn. Kelly Mahler's OT interoception curriculum is widely used in schools. | From age 6: "¿Dónde lo sientes en tu cuerpo?" |
| **Co-regulation comes before self-regulation.** Young children borrow an adult's calm nervous system. Self-regulation grows out of many repetitions of co-regulation (Harvard Center on the Developing Child; Murray et al., 2015, OPRE co-regulation reports). | 3–5 is always "Juntos" (together). The adult gets a script on every screen. |
| **Practice when calm, use when stormy.** A child in a full "red" state has little access to the thinking brain. Skills practiced in green are easier to reach in red. | The green ("verde") path is framed as "training a superpower". |
| **Play is the delivery system.** Animals, imagery, sounds and props turn an abstract instruction ("breathe slowly") into something concrete. | Each breath is an animal or object with a sound or action. |

## 2. How kids of each age understand breath

Typical resting breathing rates (pediatric norms) explain why "breathe slowly" means different things at each age:

| Age | Typical resting breaths/min | What "slow" realistically means |
|---|---|---|
| 3–5 | ~20–30 | 2–3 s in, 3–4 s out. **No breath holds.** |
| 6–8 | ~18–25 | 3 s in, 1 s pause, 4–5 s out |
| 9–12 | ~16–22 | 4 s in, short pause, 5–6 s out; 3-count box |
| 13+ | ~12–20 | Adult techniques: 4-4-4-4 box, 5-5 coherent, physiological sigh |

### 3–5 years: "Semillitas" (Piaget's preoperational stage)
- **Thinking:** concrete and magical. They can't follow "inhale for 4", but they can **smell a flower** and **blow out a candle**.
- **Emotions:** they can name about 4 basic feelings (happy, sad, mad, scared), often only with faces.
- **What works:** props (bubbles, pinwheels, feathers, a stuffed animal on the belly), sounds (roar, buzz, hiss), copying the adult, **3 breaths at most**.
- **Adult role:** essential. The adult *is* the regulation tool.
- **Avoid:** counting, holding the breath, long sessions, sitting still with eyes closed.

### 6–8 years: "Exploradores"
- **Thinking:** starting to use rules and logic (Piaget's concrete operational stage); they love collecting and repeating.
- **Emotions:** they can use colors as a code (red = angry) and name more feelings (nervous, frustrated, proud).
- **What works:** animal breaths, counting together, body maps ("where do you feel it?"), stickers and collections, short stories.
- **Adult role:** guide and play partner, gradually stepping back.

### 9–12 years: "Aventureros"
- **Thinking:** curious about how things work. "Because it's good for you" is no longer enough.
- **Emotions:** they can tell apart frustration, disappointment, embarrassment and worry, and rate intensity.
- **What works:** short "why it works" explanations, intensity scales (1–5), before/after comparison (data!), choosing between techniques, a little friendly challenge.
- **Adult role:** optional coach. They should feel some ownership.

### 13–15 and 16+: "Navegantes" / "Por mi cuenta"
- **Thinking:** abstract reasoning, identity, autonomy. They are sensitive to anything that feels babyish.
- **What works:** real technique names (box breathing, physiological sigh, coherent breathing), the science behind them, short protocols (1–5 min), tying it to their goals (exams, sports, sleep, social anxiety), journaling or tracking, and privacy.
- **Adult role:** invite, never force. From 16, fully self-guided by default.

## 3. Tools that researchers and educators already use

| Tool / program | Who / where | What it does | How we borrowed it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zones of Regulation** | Leah Kuypers, OT (2011) | 4 color zones: Blue (sad, tired), Green (calm, ready), Yellow (worried, silly, frustrated), Red (anger, terror). Kids learn their zone and a matching tool. | The color check-in. We added **morado** for fear so young kids get a dedicated "safe" path. *Note: "Zones of Regulation" is a trademark. We are "inspired by" it, not using it.* |
| **RULER / Mood Meter** | Marc Brackett, Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence | A 2-axis grid: energy (high/low) × pleasantness. Red = high energy/unpleasant, Yellow = high energy/pleasant, Blue = low/unpleasant, Green = low/pleasant. Builds emotion vocabulary. | Word chips for ages 9+, intensity slider. |
| **MindUP** | Goldie Hawn Foundation; RCT by Schonert-Reichl et al., 2015 (*Developmental Psychology*) | Classroom mindfulness with "core practice" breathing 3×/day. Grade 4–5 kids improved in executive function, stress physiology, empathy and math grades. | Short daily practice and "brain science" explanations for 9+. |
| **Kindness Curriculum** | Center for Healthy Minds (Flook et al., 2015, *Developmental Psychology*) | 12-week mindfulness program for preschoolers, including "belly buddies". Improved social competence and grades compared with controls. | Shows that even 4-year-olds benefit, if the practice is playful and short. |
| **Breathing Buddies** | Susan Kaiser Greenland, *The Mindful Child* | Kids lie down with a stuffed animal on their belly and "rock it to sleep" with their breath. | A future prop-based mini-game (see ideas). |
| **Sesame Street "Belly Breathe"** | Sesame Workshop (2013) | Elmo, a song and a monster who learns to belly-breathe when angry. | Shows that a character plus a song is enough for 3–5. |
| **Animal / yoga breaths** (lion, bee, bunny, snake) | Children's yoga tradition, e.g. *bhramari* (bee), *simhasana* (lion) | Sound and movement make the exhale longer, release tension and focus attention. | 🦁 león, 🐝 abeja, 🐰 conejito. |
| **Star / 5-finger breathing** | Widespread in school counseling | Trace the fingers of one hand: up = inhale, down = exhale. | ⭐ estrella (an SVG star path). |
| **Box breathing** | Used by military and first responders | 4 equal sides: in, hold, out, hold. | 📦 caja (3 s for 9–12, 4 s for 13+). |
| **Physiological / cyclic sigh** | Balban et al., 2023, *Cell Reports Medicine* (adults) | Double inhale + long exhale. 5 min/day improved mood more than mindfulness meditation. | 😮‍💨 suspiro (from 9; teens' first choice for red). |
| **Coherent / resonance breathing** | Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014 (HRV biofeedback) | About 6 breaths/min. | 🌊 ola. |
| **Test-anxiety breathing** | Khng, 2017 (*Cognition & Emotion*) | Kids who did deep breathing before a math test had lower anxiety and did better. | A future "before an exam" mode for 9+. |

## 4. What the game does today

**Flow:** choose age → Juntos / Por mi cuenta → "¿Cómo me siento?" (color) → words, body and intensity (by age) → an emotion-validation message → the animal breath (with an adult script in Juntos mode) → ending ritual (bear hug, rub hands, name 3 things…) → after check-in → sticker plus a before/after comparison → if still red/yellow/morado, offer an alternative breath.

### Color → animal map

| Color | Feeling | 3–8 | 9–12 | 13+ | Ending ritual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🟥 Rojo | enojo, furia, frustración | 🐻 **Oso** (alt: 🦁 León) | 🐻 Oso / 🦁 León | 😮‍💨 Suspiro doble / 🐻 Oso | **Abrazo de oso** 🤗 |
| 🟨 Amarillo | nervios, preocupación, mucha emoción | 🐝 Abeja / 🌸 Flor | 📦 Caja / 🐝 Abeja | 📦 Caja / 😮‍💨 Suspiro | Feel the buzz in your face |
| 🟪 Morado | miedo, susto, inseguridad | 🐢 Tortuga / ⭐ Estrella | same | same | "Name 3 things you see": you're safe |
| 🟦 Azul | tristeza, cansancio | 🐳 Ballena / 🐰 Conejito | same | same | Hand on heart; "do you want to tell me something?" |
| 🟩 Verde | calma, alegría | 🌸 Flor / ⭐ Estrella | ⭐ / 🌸 | 🌊 Ola / ⭐ | Name one good thing from today |

### Adaptation by age (built in)

| | 3–5 | 6–8 | 9–12 | 13–15 | 16+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mode | Always Juntos | Juntos recommended | Either | Either | Solo recommended |
| Check-in | 4 big faces | 5 colors | 5 colors + words + intensity | same | same |
| Body map | – | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Timing (in / out) | 2.5 s / ~3.5 s, no holds | 3 / ~4.5 s, 1 s pause | 4 / ~6 s | 4 / ~6.5 s | 4 / ~7 s |
| Breaths per game | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 |
| Countdown numbers | – | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| "Why it works" | – | – | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Language | Imagery ("huele la miel") | Imagery + action | Imagery + body | Technique names | Technique names |

Other features: an optional chime and a Spanish voice guide (helps kids who can't read yet), a sticker collection, auto-pause when the tab is hidden, reduced-motion support, and a parent guide with safety notes.

## 5. More game ideas for future versions

### For 3–5 (play with props and the adult)
1. **Breathing Buddy / Peluche dormilón.** The child lies down with a stuffed animal on the belly. The screen shows the animal on a wave, and "we rock it to sleep" with our breath. *Physical, calming, great at bedtime.*
2. **Sopla la burbuja (microphone game).** The phone mic detects a soft, steady blow. A bubble grows only if the exhale is gentle and long; blowing hard pops it. *Teaches control of exhale speed.* (Needs microphone permission, so ask the parent first.)
3. **Pluma flotante.** Keep a feather in the air by blowing softly (a real feather, with the app as a timer and song).
4. **Canción de la respiración.** A short song in Spanish with built-in pauses for "inhala" and "exhala", like Sesame's "Belly Breathe".
5. **El cuento que respira.** An illustrated story where the character gets angry or scared and the child helps by breathing. Each page turns only after a breath.

### For 6–8 (collecting, stories, movement)
6. **Zoológico de respiraciones.** The sticker collection grows into a zoo. Each animal "unlocks" after you practice it 3 times on calm days.
7. **El mapa del cuerpo.** Tap a body outline to color where you feel the emotion, then see it "cool down" after breathing. *Builds interoception.*
8. **Respira y muévete.** Breath plus a yoga pose: arms up on the inhale (árbol), fold down on the exhale (rana). Good for yellow/silly energy.
9. **El termómetro / volcán.** A volcano starts bubbling at the chosen intensity, and each long exhale cools the lava.
10. **Misión diaria.** One calm "green" practice per day earns a star. 7 stars = a family reward chosen together.

### For 9–12 (data, mastery, why)
11. **Detective del corazón.** With the parent's phone camera on a fingertip (photoplethysmography), show the heart rate before and after. Kids love seeing the number drop. *(Accuracy varies, so present it as an experiment, not a medical measurement.)*
12. **Antes del examen.** A 2-minute pre-test protocol (based on Khng, 2017) with a box or wave breath and a positive self-statement.
13. **Diario de emociones.** Log the color and intensity each day, with a weekly chart: "On Mondays you're often yellow… what happens on Mondays?"
14. **Elige tu herramienta.** After trying all the breaths, the kid ranks their top 3 into a "toolbox" for school.

### For 13+ (autonomy, real-life goals)
15. **Protocols by goal.** "Antes de dormir" (4-7-8 or wave, 5 min), "Antes de competir" (box), "Ataque de nervios" (cyclic sigh, 1–2 min), "Concentración" (wave, 3 min).
16. **Racha / streak tracker.** Private, no social pressure. Suggest 5 min/day for 2 weeks and compare mood ratings.
17. **Ciencia en 60 segundos.** Short explainer cards on the vagus nerve, HRV, CO₂ tolerance and why the long exhale works.
18. **Modo discreto.** Breaths you can do in class or on the bus without anyone noticing (no sounds, no movements, eyes open).

### For the whole family / the coach's program
19. **Ritual familiar.** A 3-minute family breathing ritual at dinner or bedtime. Each person picks their color and everyone does the breath of whoever is "most red".
20. **Tarjetas imprimibles.** Printable cards with each animal, the color and a one-line instruction for the fridge or classroom ("Cuando estoy rojo, respiro como oso").
21. **Coach dashboard (later).** Families share anonymous before/after data so the coach can see program outcomes.

## 6. Safety and ethics checklist

- **Never force** breathing exercises. Offer choices; eyes open is fine.
- **No breath holds for under-6s.** Only gentle, short pauses for 6–12.
- **No fast or forceful breathing** (Wim Hof-style, Kapalabhati, hyperventilation) with children.
- **Dizziness or tingling** → stop and breathe normally.
- **Asthma or respiratory conditions** → check with the pediatrician.
- **Anxiety or trauma:** focusing on the breath can *increase* distress for some people. Always offer an external anchor instead (looking at the hand, counting objects, moving).
- **Not a replacement** for mental-health care. If a child stays "red", "blue" or "morado" often, refer to a professional.
- **Privacy:** the game only stores stickers and settings on the device (localStorage). Any future data feature needs explicit parental consent (and COPPA/GDPR-K review if it collects data).
- **Trademarks:** "Zones of Regulation" and "RULER/Mood Meter" are proprietary. Describe the game as *inspired by* color-based emotional regulation, and don't use their names or artwork in marketing.
